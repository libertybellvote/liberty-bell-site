How to read The Bell

A market signal, put in political context.

The Bell answers one question: which way is the 2028 race leaning right now? Prediction markets are the live signal. Polling, the electoral map, the economy, and campaign strength tell us whether that signal makes political sense.

What the percentages mean

A 58% Democratic reading means the available evidence currently favors a Democratic win—not that the election is settled, and not that Democrats are projected to win 58% of the vote. The farther the Bell moves from 50–50, the stronger the apparent advantage.

Economic conditionsVoters’ experience of inflation, jobs, income, and growth.
Campaign fundamentalsMoney, organization, endorsements, coalition, and candidate quality.

Why markets are useful

Markets move faster than most traditional indicators and force participants to put a price on uncertainty. That makes them valuable as a real-time information signal. It does not make them infallible: thin trading, attention spikes, unclear rules, and crowd behavior can distort a price.

Why The Bell may disagree

The market board reports current prices. Our editorial read adds context: whether the movement is broad or isolated, whether polling supports it, and whether the political fundamentals have actually changed. A viral moment can move a candidate’s price without changing their path to the nomination.

Our rating language

  • Toss-up: neither party has a durable advantage.
  • Leans: one party has a visible but reversible edge.
  • Favors: several signals point in the same direction.
